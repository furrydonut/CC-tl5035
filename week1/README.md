The meaning of the word “code” has evolved across different periods–from a book of law that consolidated the power of the ruling class in Ancient Rome, to a creative medium that liberates artistic expression today. But we still have to stay critical and cautious when moving forward, as Kittler reminds us that code fundamentally remains a language of power and control, dictating how we perceive reality.

My coding professor once said that coding is basically giving a series of instructions to a powerful but dumb robot: you cannot tell it what to do without breaking the rules down into single, precise moves. As a 3D animator, I used to treat that robot as a tool to build other tools, such as scripts, extensions, and procedural setups. I had never considered code could have its artistic value, acting as a paintbrush for the digital artist. That shift in mindset felt a bit counter-intuitive to me, and I felt I was still missing the idea that bridges the technical and the cerative.

However, as I browsed the web for quotes on creative coding, I came across an artist who uses p5.js to generate geometric patterns from different cultures. I was deeply inspired by her works, and I would love to reinterpret this concept through my own cultural background using code.
